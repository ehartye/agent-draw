## Lessons for reusable craft guidance

- The initial low street shot had strong foreground scale and doorway focus, but “cinematic” plus rain introduced gray atmospheric haze and photographic wet-road bokeh. Naming a three-color palette alone did not prevent that drift. The targeted street re-generation specified printed graphic ink, flat white sky, few broad reflection cuts, hard edges and no photography/DoF/bokeh; the actual result is visibly flatter and more graphic. Both outputs are preserved for comparison.
- Three named art inks do not imply exactly three RGB values in generated raster pixels. Antialiasing, textured ink and some neutral tonal remnants remain; do not claim quantization or measured palette compliance without doing that work.
- Specify each camera, physical action and focal role separately. The set varies low street, elevated stealth alley, steep overhead stair, side-on leap, near-foreground interior and distant frontal bridge. Simply asking for six noir images could yield six interchangeable wet streets.
- Large white coat masses against black silhouettes carry a coherent recurring investigator identity across shots. Complete anatomy/pose descriptions still matter: the rooftop leap keeps opposing arm action and naturally bent knees, while the stairwell makes the gloved rail contact and overhead shoulder construction readable.
- A meaningful convergence target helps connect gaze, selected coat cuts and environmental depth. Generated scenes still need visual inspection: their architectural vanishing points are not mathematically guaranteed to coincide exactly with every red doorway.
- Concept imagery cannot establish collision geometry. Clearly drawn route, cover, gap and landing are a useful screen brief, but must be rebuilt and reviewed in the live engine before gameplay acceptance.
- The rooftop review found that a bright parapet face could still conceal where the feet should land. The targeted revision lowered the front obstruction, moved the rail behind the landing and exposed the tread as a broad plane above a contrasting face. This is a concept-level visual correction, not a collision test.
- Show full screens in the review interface. Cropping thumbnails or the main image would hide landing, boot contact or the giant foreground framing that makes the composition work.
